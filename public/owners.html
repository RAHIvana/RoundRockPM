Owner questions

What owners ask before signing

How is the management fee structured?

A monthly management fee based on collected rent, plus a leasing fee when we place a new tenant. The exact percentages depend on property type, unit count and condition, which is why we quote after seeing the property rather than posting a number that may not apply to you. Whatever the quote says is what appears on your statement — there are no fees introduced later.

Do I approve repairs?

Yes. You set a dollar threshold in the management agreement. Anything above it comes to you with the vendor quote before work begins. The only exception is a genuine emergency — an active leak, no heat in a freeze, a security or safety risk — where waiting for a callback would cause more damage than the repair itself. You are told immediately in those cases.

Do I approve tenants?

You have the final say. We screen against written criteria, present the complete report, and recommend. What we will not do is apply different standards to different applicants — Fair Housing law requires consistency, and it protects you as much as the applicant.

What happens if a tenant stops paying?

We contact them immediately, document everything in writing, and follow the notice timeline Texas law requires. Most cases resolve at that stage. If it does not, we coordinate the eviction process with your approval and keep you updated at each step, along with what it is likely to cost and how long it will take.

Can you take over a property that already has tenants?

Yes, and it is common. We review the existing lease, introduce ourselves to the residents, move rent collection and maintenance requests onto our systems, and document current condition so the next move-out is not a dispute. Existing lease terms stay in force until renewal.

How often do you inspect?

Detailed documentation at move-in and move-out, plus periodic condition checks during the tenancy with appropriate notice. You get photos. The point is to catch a slow leak or a neglected filter while it is still a small line item.
