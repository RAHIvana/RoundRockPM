Before you apply

Rental application criteria

We publish our standards and apply them identically to every applicant, as Fair Housing law requires. Specific thresholds can vary by property — the listing and the application will state them.

RequirementWhat we look for
Every adult appliesAnyone 18 or older who will live in the home completes an application and is screened.
Verifiable incomeGross household income is assessed against the rent, evidenced by pay stubs, an offer letter, tax returns or bank records.
Credit historyA full credit report is reviewed. Payment patterns and outstanding housing debt matter more than a single number.
Rental historyPrevious landlords are contacted. Outstanding balances or unresolved evictions are a problem.
Background checkCriminal background is reviewed individually, considering nature, seriousness and how long ago it was.
IdentificationGovernment-issued photo ID for each applicant.
PetsPet policy varies by property. Where pets are allowed, a pet screening and additional deposit or fee may apply. Assistance animals are handled per federal and Texas law and are not pets.

Resident FAQ

Common resident questions

When is rent due and what happens if I am late?

Rent is due on the date stated in your lease. Late fees and the grace period, if any, are set out there as well and are applied consistently. If something has gone wrong, tell us before the due date — a conversation early is far better than silence, and it is usually the difference between a workable plan and a formal notice.

How do I get my security deposit back?

Give proper written notice, return the home clean and undamaged beyond normal wear and tear, return all keys and remotes, and leave a forwarding address in writing. Texas law then sets the timeline for refunding the deposit with an itemized statement of any deductions. The move-in condition report we took photos of is what we compare against, so it protects you too.

Can I paint, mount a TV or make changes?

Ask first, in writing. Many small changes are approved. Undocumented alterations are the most common cause of deposit deductions, and a quick message avoids that entirely.

What am I responsible for maintaining?

Typically air filters, light bulbs, smoke detector batteries, lawn care where the lease says so, and keeping drains clear of what does not belong in them. Your lease is specific. Anything structural, mechanical or beyond routine upkeep is ours — report it rather than attempting the repair yourself.

Can I renew my lease?

We start renewal conversations before expiry so nobody is left guessing. Renewal terms reflect current market rent, and good residents are worth keeping — that is not just a nice sentiment, it is cheaper than a turnover for the owner.
